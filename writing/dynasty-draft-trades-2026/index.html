Reading the room, then trading for my timeline

By Michael ChenPublished 2026-11-11

My plan going into the dynasty auction was to get a vibe of the room. I wanted to see how expensive people were going to be and adapt, rather than force one approach the whole way through.

People bid really high on the first few players. I took a more casual depth approach and tried to get deals.

Starting with Johnson and Giddey

My first buy was Jalen Johnson for $225. He was the 18th sale of the auction, right after Cade Cunningham went for $304 and before Ryan Rollins went for $38.

Then I got Josh Giddey for $153 at sale #21. Stephen Curry had just gone for $47, and Giannis Antetokounmpo went next for $221. Those numbers are the order players sold, not rankings or snake-draft picks.

Johnson and Giddey appealed to me because they're young and I think they have very high potential. Once I had Johnson, I wanted to lean toward players around 24–28 rather than build around older stars.

Curry's age put me off. With Giannis, my concern was how well he'd age.

Towns at $80

Towns was 30, and I thought $80 was pretty cheap for him. He was sale #28, right after I bought Austin Reaves for $75 and before Cameron Boozer went for $250.

The rest of my auction included Walker Kessler for $94, Franz Wagner for $91, Trae Young for $70 and Jaylen Brown for $51. I finished with 25 players and spent $987 of the $1,000 budget.

Fears for Alexander-Walker

On October 28, I sent Jeremiah Fears, my 2027 second and my 2028 first for Nickeil Alexander-Walker.

I sent Fears away before he started to pop off because he didn't match my timeline. Alexander-Walker is 28. I like his three-point shot and his defense, and I think that should age well.

Fears had cost me $6 at sale #101, between Immanuel Quickley at $19 and Kyshawn George at $14. The day after the trade, Fears put up 22 points, eight rebounds and 13 assists. I didn't have that game to look at when I made the deal. Alexander-Walker later had 20 points, four assists, two steals and two blocks on November 9.

Kessler and picks for three known players

On November 11, I sent Kessler, my 2027 first, my 2028 second and my 2029 first for Darius Garland, Tyler Herro and Jaren Jackson Jr.

I wanted more known quantities in that 26–27 age range. Garland and Herro are 26. Jackson is 27. All three had already been All-Stars, including selections in 2025.

I don't value my future picks very highly for this team because I plan to contend for the next three years. I already have Chris Cenac Jr. and Karim Lopez in my taxi slots. I don't feel like I need more picks right now.

There are health questions with the players coming back. Utah reported Jackson's right-knee MCL tear and a six-week reevaluation in October. That's a reevaluation, not a promised return date. Herro also missed November 10 with a bruised right knee.

I knew about the injuries, but I was fine taking on Jackson because I have IR spots. Injuries happen. I saw Herro as day-to-day, so that didn't really matter to me in the deal.

The second trade only happened on November 11, so there's no post-trade result to judge yet. I want to contend for the next three years. We'll see how it goes.
